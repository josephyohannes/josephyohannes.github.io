CRM Sales Pipeline
Analysis.

What explains the differences in sales revenue? I investigated 8,800 opportunities from Maven Analytics' fictional B2B CRM dataset, comparing agents, products, managers, regions and customer sectors.

Main takeaway: Higher revenue often came alongside more opportunities and a higher-value product mix, even when overall Win Rates were similar.

01 / THE QUESTION

What drives stronger sales performance?

I wanted to understand what sat behind the revenue rankings: whether the differences reflected opportunity volume, conversion, product mix or a combination of these. I organised the investigation around four business questions.

01Sales agentsWhat distinguishes top performers?
02ProductsWhich products have the greatest commercial impact?
03Managers & regionsWhat explains differences between teams?
04Customer sectorsHow closely is revenue linked to opportunity volume?
02 / MY APPROACH

Preparing the data before analysing it.

I worked with four related tables: sales opportunities, products, customer accounts and sales teams. Each row of the sales_pipeline fact table represents one opportunity. I prepared the data in Power Query and used Power BI relationships and DAX measures to compare performance.

01

Data cleaning — Corrected GTXPro to GTX Pro in Power Query. The mismatch had caused sales to appear under a blank product row; matching the names restored the link to the products table.

02

Data quality — Investigated 1,425 opportunities without a customer sector. All were still open, so I retained them in the dataset but excluded the blank category from the sector comparison.

03

Measures and analysis — Used Won Revenue and Won Deals to measure successful outcomes, Win Rate to compare conversion among won and lost deals, and Average Won Deal Value to compare value per win. Opportunity Mix % showed each product's share of a group's opportunities.

View the DAX behind Opportunity Mix %

I used this measure to compare the share of opportunities for each product within an agent or manager group. Removing the product filter gives the denominator across all products while keeping the other filters, such as the agent group, in place.

Opportunity Mix % =
DIVIDE(
    [Total Opportunities],
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Opportunities],
        REMOVEFILTERS(products)
    )
)

This makes groups with different opportunity totals easier to compare. The full notes include the other measures and the calculated Agent Group column used to compare Darcel with the other agents.

03 / KEY FINDINGS

The patterns behind the performance.

FINDING 01 SALES AGENTS

Higher revenue didn't mean a higher overall Win Rate.

Darcel Schlecht generated the most Won Revenue at around $1.15M. His Win Rate was almost identical to the company average, but he handled 65.6% more opportunities than the next-highest agent and had a much larger share of higher-value products.

His overall Average Won Deal Value was $3,304.34, compared with $2,276.25 for the other agents. I then compared the same products and found that Average Won Deal Values were generally similar. This suggests the higher overall average mainly reflected which products he sold, rather than consistently closing the same products at much higher values.

What this suggests: Darcel's revenue lead appears to be linked more to opportunity volume and product mix than to a much higher overall conversion rate.

FINDING 02 PRODUCTS

A smaller share of opportunities generated most of the revenue.

GTX Pro and GTX Plus Pro generated around 61% of total Won Revenue despite accounting for only around 28% of opportunities. Lower-value products could have high opportunity volumes without generating the same revenue.

What this suggests: Product value matters alongside opportunity volume. Price alone is not enough without a sufficient number of opportunities.

FINDING 03 MANAGERS & REGIONS

Product mix helped explain differences between managers.

West was the highest-revenue region, while Melvin Marxen generated the most revenue among managers. I compared Melvin with Dustin Brinkmann because both were in the Central region, yet Dustin generated less revenue despite a slightly higher Win Rate. Their product mix showed an important difference: 36.70% of Melvin's opportunities involved the three highest-priced products, versus 10.61% for Dustin.

What this suggests: Differences in revenue cannot be explained by Win Rate alone. Opportunity volume and exposure to higher-value products both appear important.

FINDING 04 CUSTOMER SECTORS

Revenue broadly increased with opportunity volume.

Retail generated the highest Won Revenue at approximately $1.87M and also had the most opportunities. Its Win Rate and Average Won Deal Value were both close to the overall averages. The scatter chart showed a clear positive relationship between opportunity volume and revenue across sectors.

What this suggests: Retail's lead appears largely linked to having more opportunities, rather than an unusually strong Win Rate.

04 / RECOMMENDATIONS

What the business could investigate next.

Based on the findings, I would focus on three areas before making changes to the sales strategy.

01

Review opportunity allocation

Sales managers could review allocation by product and customer type, checking whether differences reflect customer demand, account ownership or team capacity. If a small change is trialled, monitor Won Revenue, Win Rate and Average Won Deal Value alongside opportunity volume and product mix.

02

Investigate higher-value product opportunities

Explore whether more opportunities could be generated for GTX Pro and GTX Plus Pro, and why some teams have greater exposure to these products.

03

Assess team performance in context

Consider opportunity volume and product mix alongside Win Rate, and investigate strong performance on particular products that other agents might learn from.

Further area to investigate: Finance had the lowest sector Win Rate at 61.17% but one of the highest Average Won Deal Values. Understanding why its conversion is lower could be useful.

05 / LIMITATIONS

What the data doesn't tell us.

Although opportunity volume, product mix and Won Revenue were closely linked in the analysis, that doesn't prove these factors alone caused the differences in performance. More information about customer demand, opportunity quality and how leads are allocated would be needed before recommending major changes.
